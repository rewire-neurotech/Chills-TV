Why chills —
and what the science says

ChillsTV draws on over a decade of research on aesthetic chills, peak emotional experience, and the neuroscience of reward — spanning MIT Media Lab, Harvard Medical School, and the Institute for Advanced Consciousness Studies. Below are the key papers.

Cognitive, Affective & Behavioral Neuroscience  ·  2023
The neurobiology of aesthetic chills
Schoeller, Jain, Pizzagalli & Reggente

A comprehensive review of what happens in the brain when you experience chills. Aesthetic chills activate the dopaminergic mesolimbic reward pathway — the same system involved in food, sex, and drugs of abuse — but through a distinct mechanism tied to prediction error and emotional learning. The review synthesizes neuroimaging, lesion, pharmacological, and electrophysiological evidence to show that chills engage a distributed network spanning the ventral tegmental area, nucleus accumbens, insula, and default mode network. Crucially, it demonstrates that individual differences in reward sensitivity, interoception, and personality predict who gets chills — and how strongly.

Key findings
Chills are mediated by dopamine release in the striatum (confirmed via PET and pharmacological blockade)  ·  They co-occur with autonomic markers: piloerection, skin conductance spikes, heart rate deceleration  ·  The default mode network shows increased coupling during chills — linking self-referential processing to reward  ·  Individual differences in openness, absorption, and reward sensitivity are reliable predictors

Scientific Data  ·  Nature  ·  2023
ChillsDB: a validated stimulus library
Schoeller et al.

The 40 videos in ChillsTV come from ChillsDB — an open-access, peer-reviewed database of audiovisual stimuli that reliably elicit aesthetic chills. These were not hand-picked. They were mined algorithmically from YouTube and Reddit using NLP, scanning comments for somatic language ("frisson," "goosebumps," "chills," "spine tingling"). Each candidate was then independently validated across thousands of participants on Prolific, with embedded attention checks and psychometric controls.

The result is a rigorous, reproducible stimulus set with published chills rates for each clip — meaning when we match you to a video, the prediction is grounded in empirical population data, not a guess.

Stimulus coverage
204 initial candidates mined from YouTube and Reddit  ·  40 validated stimuli confirmed across 3,500+ independent raters  ·  Covers music (orchestral, choral, pop), cinematic moments, speeches, and nature scenes  ·  Released under CC BY 4.0

PNAS Nexus  ·  2024
Predicting individual differences in peak emotional response
Schoeller, Christov-Moore, Lynch, Diot & Reggente · N = 2,937

This study demonstrates that chills responses can be predicted from personality and emotional state variables with meaningful accuracy. Key predictors include emotional absorption (MODTAS), Kama Muta proneness (KAMF), pre-stimulus arousal, and personality dimensions — the same variables collected in our questionnaire. The study also identifies distinct population subgroups with dramatically different chills rates, reinforcing that chills is not random but deeply structured by individual psychology.

Key findings
Chills rates range from near-zero to 88% depending on individual personality profile  ·  Kama Muta (KAMF) and absorption (MODTAS) are among the strongest individual predictors  ·  Pre-stimulus emotional state (arousal, mood) significantly modulates response  ·  Individual differences account for more variance than stimulus type alone

Frontiers in Neuroscience  ·  2023
Aesthetic chills cause an emotional drift in valence and arousal
Jain, Schoeller, Horowitz, Hu, Yan, Salomon & Maes

Across 103 participants with depressive symptoms, those who experienced chills during exposure to ChillsDB stimuli showed a significant increase in reward learning bias compared to those who did not (p = 0.004). This is direct evidence that chills temporarily shift emotional state — increasing both positive valence and physiological arousal — and may offer a non-pharmacological pathway to improve reward processing in anhedonia.

Scientific Reports  ·  Nature  ·  2020
Augmenting aesthetic chills using a wearable prosthesis improves their downstream effects on reward and social cognition
Haar, Jain, Schoeller et al.

When chills were amplified using a wearable device that enhances the physical sensation of piloerection (goosebumps), participants showed stronger downstream effects — including improved social cognition scores and heightened reward sensitivity. This study extends the therapeutic and augmentation potential of chills beyond passive exposure.

Frontiers in Psychology  ·  2016
Aesthetic chills: Knowledge-acquisition, meaning-making and aesthetic emotions
Schoeller & Perlovsky

One of the foundational theoretical papers in this line of research. Proposes that aesthetic chills signal a moment of unusually rapid knowledge acquisition — a fit between an ascending signal and a newly resolved internal model. This predictive coding account connects chills to learning, curiosity, and the satiation of a fundamental need for meaning, and remains influential across the field.
